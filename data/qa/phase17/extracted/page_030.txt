Phukan stated that the visits have contributed to a ‘volatile situation’ and questioned whether external actors are attempting to disrupt peace in the
region. He emphasized that while developmental activities are welcome, they must be free from political motives. ‘People are aware of the political
nature of CJP’s actions,’ Phukan said, adding that AASU remains committed to working for Assam’s people without such agendas [Source 9].
The CJP campaign began with visits to government schools in Palashbari and Dispur constituencies, where a team led by co-convener Ashutosh Ranka
evaluated challenges faced by students, teachers, and schools. The party has announced plans to expand these visits across Assam and the Northeast
[Source 9].
The initiative follows a recent regional volunteers’ meeting in Guwahati on September 27, which was disrupted by Assam Police. Around 35 CJP
members, including Ranka, were detained ahead of the gathering due to lack of prior permission. The detainees protested with a hunger strike before
being released later that night [Source 9].
AASU’s criticism underscores broader tensions over CJP’s activities, with Phukan urging the public to recognize ‘the force’ allegedly seeking to
destabilize Assam [Source 9].
