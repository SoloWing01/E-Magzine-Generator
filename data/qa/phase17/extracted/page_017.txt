During his visit on Friday, Bhalla inspected coffee plantation sites and the Hi-Tech Coffee Washing Station, engaging directly with farmers and
members of the Tuophema cluster. He highlighted that the Coffee USP Project extends beyond mere plantation initiatives, aiming to serve as an
integrated livelihood and economic development programme. The goal is to empower villages, societies, and clusters by maximizing the value derived
from the region’s natural and human resources.
Bhalla stressed that sustainable livelihoods for coffee farmers depend on more than just cultivation. For the sector to become commercially viable and
competitive, he noted the necessity of good road connectivity, modern processing machinery, efficient post-harvest infrastructure, quality assurance,
value addition, and strong market and export linkages. These elements are critical to ensuring the sector’s growth and profitability.
The NEC Secretary expressed hope that the project would significantly boost the income of farmers and communities, underscoring that achieving this
requires collaboration among the Government of India, the Nagaland Government, the Coffee Board, local administration, farmers, technical
institutions, and the private sector. He urged the Nagaland Government and the Coffee Board to collaborate with Central agricultural institutions to
identify suitable intercrops and complementary agricultural activities. Such diversification, Bhalla stated, would enhance land productivity, provide
additional income sources for farmers, and strengthen the overall economic viability of coffee cultivation.
Additionally, Bhalla called for the local administration to engage with leading industrial houses, investors, and exporters to facilitate mechanisation and
automation in the coffee sector through financial and institutional arrangements. This collective effort, he emphasized, aligns with broader strategies
evolved by the Chief Ministers of the North Eastern States to substantially enhance farmers’ incomes.
