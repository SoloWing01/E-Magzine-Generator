Roaming cattle and roadside garbage pose civic
challenges in Lumding 
The Assam Tribune 2026-10-04T06:06:13
The presence of large animals on roads poses risks to both motorists and pedestrians. Two-wheeler riders, in particular,
may face sudden evasive maneuvers by cattle, increasing the likelihood of falls and injuries. Pedestrians also struggle
when cattle block roadside passages or narrow sections of the road. 
Local citizens have expressed the need for municipal or local authorities to address the issue systematically. They suggest
identifying areas where cattle frequently roam and checking whether existing rules are being violated. Coordination with
animal welfare agencies may also be necessary to ensure that measures are implemented without harming the animals. 
A temporary solution, such as removing cattle from specific stretches, is not sufficient. Instead, a long-term approach
involving recurring location identification, communication with animal owners, and enforcement of regulations could
help resolve the underlying problem. 
The situation in Lumding emphasizes the importance of responsible animal ownership and consistent civic oversight.
Public roads must remain accessible to all users, while regular maintenance of roadside areas is essential to prevent waste
accumulation.
