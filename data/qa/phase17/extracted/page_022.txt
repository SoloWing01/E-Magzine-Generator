Haokip’s disappearance came to light in the evening of Saturday, as heavy rainfall had kept many villagers away from their fields. The deceased was
known to frequently spend nights in his field hut, and villagers launched a search after discovering the burnt structure and his absence. The search
continued until dark but failed to locate him, and operations were suspended overnight due to darkness and security concerns [Source 31].
The Kuki Youth Organisation (KYO), Leimakhong Block, had earlier called for Haokip’s immediate and safe return, urging authorities and security
agencies to intensify the search and take action against those responsible. Meanwhile, the Ariang (Khunkhu) Naga Village Authority issued a
clarification denying any involvement in Haokip’s disappearance. The statement emphasized that the two villages—Khunkho Kuki and Ariang
(Khunkhu) Naga—have maintained peaceful and cordial relations for decades and appealed against unverified allegations, warning that such claims
could escalate distrust and tension between the communities [Source 31].
The village authority also noted that movement restrictions had been imposed on its residents since August 14 as a precautionary measure following an
earlier incident involving Naga villages. The incident involving Haokip’s death occurs amid renewed tensions between the Kuki and Naga communities
in Manipur, particularly in Kangpokpi and neighboring areas, where reports of abductions, hostage-taking, and attacks involving civilians from both
groups have raised concerns over retaliatory violence [Source 31].
Security forces continued search and security operations in the area, but further details surrounding Haokip’s death and the circumstances leading to his
abduction remain under investigation.
