Assam CM Highlights Land Shortage as Key
Industrial Hurdle; Govt Pushes for Land Banks
and Estates 
The Assam Tribune 2026-09-10T09:09:06
"We need land. Many industries want to come to Assam, but due to the absence of suitable land, they could not come. So,
now we are trying to create land banks. Wherever there is land, we will take industries there," Sarma said, underlining the
urgency of the situation. The focus, he added, is on identifying land near railway stations and highways to facilitate the
establishment of industrial estates. 
"To create industrial estates, we need suitable land near railway stations and highways. Wherever we find land in such
locations, we have decided to build industrial estates there," the Chief Minister stated, outlining the government’s strategy
to enhance industrial infrastructure. 
The inauguration of the PepsiCo facility, which is expected to generate over 200 direct and 500 indirect employment
opportunities—with around 75% of the workforce comprising women—marks a significant milestone for Assam’s
industrial sector. Sarma highlighted that approximately 120 women will receive digital literacy training as part of the
initiative. The project, which was laid out by Sarma and then Industries Minister Chandra Mohan Patowary on September
3, 2023, is poised to transform Assam’s manufacturing landscape, with products from the facility set to be distributed
nationwide. 
The facility’s operations will also bolster local agriculture, particularly the potato sector. PepsiCo currently requires
around 30,000 metric tonnes of potatoes annually, with projections indicating a rise to 70,000 metric tonnes by 2030. To
support this demand, the state government, PepsiCo, and the State Bank of India have collaborated to facilitate the
procurement of specialized potato seeds for farmers through Kisan Credit Cards, benefiting around 5,000 farmers and
their families. Additionally, a Rs 35-crore cold storage facility with a capacity of 10,000 tonnes is being established to
strengthen the potato supply chain. 
Sarma stressed the importance of expanding industries within Assam to reduce the state’s workforce dependence on
employment opportunities outside the state. He cited the Shraddhanjali scheme, which has, in some months, brought back
as many as 50 bodies of Assamese workers who died while working in other states, including Karnataka, Telangana,
Maharashtra, and Kerala. "To prevent such situations, we need to establish industries and create employment
opportunities within Assam so that our people do not have to leave the state in search of work," he said.
