The movement of large animals on public roads creates unexpected obstacles for drivers, particularly for two-wheeler riders who may face sudden
evasive maneuvers that could lead to falls and injuries. Pedestrians also encounter difficulties when cattle block roadside passages or narrow sections of
the road.
Local citizens have expressed the need for municipal or local authorities to investigate the issue, identify high-risk areas where cattle frequently roam,
and assess whether existing rules are being violated. They suggest that coordination with animal welfare agencies could help implement measures to
address the problem without harming the animals.
A long-term solution would require more than temporary removals of cattle from specific stretches. Instead, identifying recurring locations, engaging
with animal owners, and enforcing applicable regulations could help resolve the underlying issue. The situation in Lumding underscores the
importance of responsible animal ownership and consistent civic oversight to ensure public roads remain safe and accessible for all users.
