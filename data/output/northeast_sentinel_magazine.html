Guwahati Railway Station Expands Capacity with Major Infrastructure Projects

The Assam Tribune 2026-10-04T07:25:14

One of the key initiatives is the construction of the Kamakhya-Guwahati third line, a 5.74-kilometer project sanctioned at a cost of Rs 467 crore. Scheduled for completion by 2028, this additional line aims to increase capacity, reduce congestion, and improve train operations for passengers and residents of Kamrup Metro district.

Another significant project is the Agthori-Kamakhya double line, which includes the second Saraighat Rail-cum-Road Bridge. The Rs 1,474 crore project spans 7.20 kilometers, featuring a 1.3-kilometer bridge that will carry a double-line railway track alongside a three-lane road. This infrastructure is expected to enhance rail and road connectivity across the Brahmaputra while easing pressure on the existing Saraighat Bridge.

The development of a Mega Coaching Complex at New Guwahati is also underway, with a sanctioned cost of Rs 227 crore. This facility will bolster train terminal and maintenance capabilities in the region. Additionally, the redevelopment of Guwahati and Kamakhya railway stations is in progress, focusing on modern amenities and improved accessibility. Guwahati Railway Station is being transformed into a green-building facility, incorporating solar power, waste management, and water management systems.

To address environmental and wildlife concerns, the Kamakhya-Azara Elevated Corridor is being constructed. This project includes an elevated viaduct along the stretch, including sections near Deepor Beel, to ensure safe wildlife movement while maintaining efficient rail connectivity. The remaining 13.25-kilometer Azara-Kamakhya double-line stretch is targeted for completion by July 2028.

The NFR is also enhancing freight and logistics infrastructure through Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals. The GCT at Baihata, located near Guwahati, has already been completed, improving freight-handling and logistics operations in the region. These projects collectively aim to expand railway capacity, reduce congestion, and enhance the efficiency of train services around Guwahati.

Guwahati’s 800-Run ODI: Did the Pitch Serve Cricket’s Core?

The Assam Tribune 2026-10-01T12:34:04

The game saw West Indies post 405 for seven, their highest ODI total, before India chased it down with eight wickets in hand. While the spectacle of 800-plus runs and multiple centuries dominated headlines, critics like India head coach Gautam Gambhir argued that the pitch failed to create a meaningful contest between bat and ball. "You talk about cricket, but this is not cricket, this is only batting," Gambhir told Star Sports after the match, emphasizing that a good ODI should test all three disciplines equally.

Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma, however, celebrated the pitch’s batting-friendly nature, calling it "an absolute delight" on social media. His perspective reflects a growing debate: Should a pitch be judged by its ability to produce high scores, or by its capacity to challenge batters, bowlers, and fielders alike?

India’s batting coach Sitanshu Kotak added another layer to the discussion, questioning whether modern batting standards—powered by fitness, technology, and aggressive strokeplay—should influence pitch expectations. He noted that no pitch can guarantee a specific outcome, but the Guwahati surface’s consistency in producing high totals (including India’s 373 in 2023 and a 322 chase in 2018) raises concerns about its balance.

The core issue lies in whether the pitch rewarded skill across all aspects of the game or merely amplified batting dominance. If 400 runs no longer feel challenging and bowlers struggle to find traction, the surface may have lost its ability to create uncertainty—the very essence of cricket. The Guwahati ODI’s record books may shine, but the debate over what constitutes a "good" pitch remains unresolved.
