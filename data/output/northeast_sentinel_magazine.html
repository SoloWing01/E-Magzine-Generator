To address rising demand and operational challenges, the NFR has launched multiple infrastructure projects across Guwahati and its surrounding areas. One key initiative is the **Kamakhya-Guwahati third line**, a 5.74-kilometer stretch sanctioned at a cost of Rs 467 crore. Scheduled for completion by 2028, this project aims to ease congestion, enhance capacity, and improve train operations for passengers in Kamrup Metro district [Source 15].

Another major undertaking is the **Agthori-Kamakhya double line**, combined with the construction of a **second Saraighat Rail-cum-Road Bridge**. Redevelopment efforts are also transforming Guwahati and Kamakhya railway stations. Guwahati Railway Station is being modernized with an IT park and designed under green-building principles, incorporating solar power, waste management, and water conservation measures [Source 15]. Meanwhile, the **Kamakhya-Azara Elevated Corridor** project includes a 13.25-kilometer double-line stretch, with the remaining portion targeted for completion by July 2028. This elevated viaduct, particularly through the Deepor Beel area, aims to balance railway efficiency with wildlife conservation, ensuring safe passage for elephants and other species [Source 15].

The NFR is also enhancing freight and logistics capabilities through **Gati Shakti Cargo Terminals (GCTs)**. The GCT at Baihata, near Guwahati, has already been completed, improving freight-handling and logistics operations in the region [Source 15].

These projects collectively seek to expand railway capacity, reduce congestion, and improve the resilience of train operations in and around Guwahati [Source 15].

The Assam Tribune 2026-10-01T12:34:04 Article 34

The sheer volume of runs—**800-plus**—dominated headlines, but the lack of a competitive contest between bat and ball raised concerns about the pitch’s suitability for ODI cricket. While spectators and officials like **Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma** praised the surface, calling it *“an absolute delight”*, critics argued that the pitch failed to provide a balanced challenge for bowlers. **India head coach Gautam Gambhir** directly questioned the match’s fairness, stating, *“You talk about cricket, but this is not cricket, this is only batting.”* His point underscored the broader debate: should a pitch be judged by its ability to produce high scores, or by its capacity to create a **meaningful contest** across all disciplines?

The Guwahati pitch’s batting-friendly reputation was not new. In its **two previous ODIs** at the venue, India chased **322 in 2018** and posted **373 in 2023**, reinforcing the perception that Barsapara Stadium leans heavily toward batters. The latest match amplified this tendency, with bowlers struggling to extract movement or pace, leaving them with *“too little to work with”*, as Gambhir noted. The result was a game where **406 runs** were chased with **eight wickets intact** and **39 balls remaining**, further exposing the pitch’s inability to reward bowling efforts.

India’s batting coach, **Sitanshu Kotak**, offered a counterargument, suggesting that modern batting standards—powered by fitness, technology, and aggressive strokeplay—should factor into the discussion. He questioned whether pitches could realistically be engineered to produce predetermined scores, given the evolution of the game. However, the core issue remained: if a pitch reduces bowling to irrelevance, does it still serve the spirit of ODI cricket?

The debate hinges on a simple question: **What defines a ‘good’ ODI pitch?** Some argue it should prioritize entertainment, delivering boundaries and centuries for spectators. Others insist it must balance bat and ball, ensuring bowlers have a chance to influence the game. The Guwahati match highlighted this tension—**spectacular runs versus sporting contest**—leaving fans and experts to reconsider whether modern cricket’s emphasis on high scores comes at the cost of traditional competition.

As cricket continues to evolve, the Guwahati ODI serves as a case study in the clash between tradition and innovation. The pitch may have delighted batters, but its lack of challenge for bowlers forces a reckoning: **Is a ‘good’ pitch one that entertains, or one that tests all facets of the game?**
